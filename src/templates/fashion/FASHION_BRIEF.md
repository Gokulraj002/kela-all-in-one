# ATELIER — Saree & Fashion Category: Industry Research Brief (Phase 1)

**Purpose:** Ground the 10 fashion designs (design-01 through design-10) in researched industry conventions, then define a UNIQUE direction for each. Research sources include Indian ethnic-wear e-commerce UX studies, handloom craft documentation (GI-tag clusters), bridal couture booking patterns, WhatsApp-commerce behavior in Indian apparel, size-and-fit transparency studies, and slow-fashion storytelling conventions.

**Standing truths for the category:**
- Fashion sites convert through clarity of the garment: drape photography (how the saree falls on a real body), lookbook context (how it looks in daylight, at a wedding, on the move), and multiple angles beat flat product shots. A saree a buyer cannot see draped is a saree she will not buy.
- Trust converts premium and bridal buyers: craft provenance (loom cluster, weave name, GI tag, zari purity), fabric specifics (thread count, GSM, silk mark certification), and transparent pricing ("₹48,500, handloom, 6.3m with blouse piece") beat romantic copy. Specificity is the trust currency of Indian ethnic wear.
- Indian ethnic-wear buyers expect conversation before conversion: WhatsApp enquiry patterns ("Is this available in maroon?", "What is the blouse fabric?", "Do you ship to Dubai?") are the dominant pre-purchase channel for boutiques and mid-premium labels. Every design must make the WhatsApp/enquiry path visible within seconds, not buried in a contact page.
- Bridal and occasion wear convert through appointments: trousseau consultations, trial rooms, video-call appointments for NRI families, and occasion timelines (engagement → sangeet → wedding) are the funnel. A bridal site without a bookable appointment flow is a brochure.
- Never ship a product without fabric details, never hide the price behind "enquire", never let decorative motion slow the path to a price, a drape photo, or the WhatsApp button.

**Interaction inventory (fashion-specific, keep subtle and premium):** drape viewer / angle switcher (front, drape detail, border macro); fabric filter by weave and cluster; zari/weave provenance timeline; occasion calendar (festival/wedding-season edits); bridal appointment scheduler; trousseau builder; colorway switcher; size-and-fit guide; WhatsApp deep-link enquiry per product; scroll-driven lookbook chapters. Each design below uses at most two, assigned where they serve conversion.

## Cluster A — Craft / Heritage (provenance-driven, trust-first)

### design-01-heritage — Heritage Handloom House
- **Working name (suggested):** Vrinda Weaves / heritage label suggestion: "Kalaam Weaves"
- **Audience:** 35–65, connoisseurs of handloom, NRI buyers, collectors, gift buyers of Kanchipuram, Banarasi, and Chanderi sarees. They buy the story of the weave and want proof it is real.
- **Conversion goal:** Buy a handloom saree online with full provenance confidence; secondary: visit the store/looms.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — a weaver's hands at the loom, documentary frame, house name and "Since 1978" lineage. 2. The clusters — GI-tag craft clusters (Kanchipuram, Banaras, Chanderi) as cards with weave signatures. 3. Craft documentary — the loom-to-saree journey in scroll chapters (dyeing, warping, weaving, zari). 4. The collection — sarees with weave badges (GI tag, Silk Mark, zari purity). 5. Meet the weavers — artisan portraits with names and villages. 6. Provenance & care — certificates, Silk Mark, wash/care guidance. 7. Visit / WhatsApp enquiry. 8. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Editorial top bar: wordmark, Clusters, Craft, Collection, Weavers, "Enquire on WhatsApp" CTA. Mobile: sticky enquiry button.
- **Key interactions:** Weave-cluster explorer (click a cluster, see its signature motifs, zari type, price band); scroll-driven loom documentary with chapter transitions and macro weave photography. Motion stays documentary — never flashy.
- **Content blocks:** Cluster cards (loom count, signature motif, GI tag number); weave process chapters with real numbers (days on loom, thread count, zari purity); weaver portraits and quotes; certification badges (Silk Mark, Handloom Mark); care guide per fabric.
- **Mobile notes:** Clusters become a horizontal snap-scroll; documentary chapters stack; WhatsApp button always one tap away; certification badges visible on product cards.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — weaver's hands on a wooden loom in Kanchipuram, shafts of daylight, documentary grain. 2. Product — Kanchipuram silk saree draped on a model in temple light. 3. Product — Banarasi brocade close drape, zari catching light. 4. Product — Chanderi saree in daylight, sheer texture visible. 5. Detail — macro of loom threads / zari buttis, shallow depth of field.
- **Differentiation:** Craft documentary as commerce. Loom-first imagery, GI-tag data, weaver protagonists, certificate-forward product cards. The site a collector bookmarks — proof, not adjectives.

### design-05-slow — Sustainable Slow Fashion
- **Working name (suggested):** Mati Studio (earthy editorial label)
- **Audience:** 28–48, conscious consumers, design professionals, slow-living readers. They buy fewer, better pieces and read the maker story before the price.
- **Conversion goal:** Buy into the philosophy — first purchase from the core collection; secondary: newsletter / maker-story signup.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — natural-dye vats and indigo hands, earthy editorial frame, one quiet line. 2. The philosophy — slow made, natural dyes, fair artisan wages, one-page manifesto. 3. The makers — artisan portraits from dye and loom villages. 4. The collection — 8–10 timeless pieces with dye and weave data on every card. 5. Dye journal — indigo, madder, pomegranate rind; process notes. 6. Impact — wages, water, waste numbers stated plainly. 7. WhatsApp / shop. 8. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Ultra-quiet top bar: wordmark, Philosophy, Makers, Collection, Journal. No sticky shouting; a small floating enquiry link.
- **Key interactions:** Dye-journal explorer (click a dye source, see the shade range and process days); maker cards with wage-transparency notes. Breathing fades only — the calmest design in the category.
- **Content blocks:** Manifesto; maker portraits with village and craft role; per-product dye/process badges (natural dye, days of work, artisan hours); impact numbers (artisan families, water saved, dye sources); journal entries on dye and season.
- **Mobile notes:** Single calm column; maker cards stack; product badges stay legible; impact section uses large readable figures.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — indigo dye vats at dawn, hands lifting cloth, earthy tones. 2. Product — naturally dyed cotton saree on a model in field light. 3. Product — handloom kurta set, madder-dyed, flat editorial. 4. Product — jamdani weave drape, daylight detail. 5. Detail — macro of hand-spun yarn / dye-stained hands.
- **Differentiation:** Ethics as the product. Natural-dye journal, wage transparency, maker protagonists, manifesto-first. The polar opposite of the festive loud one — restraint is the luxury.

## Cluster B — Occasion / Luxury (ceremony-driven, appointment + aspiration)

### design-03-bridal — Bridal Couture
- **Working name (suggested):** Sindoor Atelier (bridal couture house)
- **Audience:** 24–40, brides and their families, NRI families planning destination weddings, trousseau buyers. High spend, high anxiety, decision by appointment and family consensus.
- **Conversion goal:** Book a bridal appointment (in-store or video call). Secondary: build a trousseau shortlist.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — cinematic bridal lehenga, opulent ceremonial frame, "Book your bridal appointment" CTA. 2. The bridal edit — lehengas and bridal sarees by ceremony (engagement, sangeet, wedding, reception). 3. Trousseau builder — pick looks across ceremonies, save a shortlist. 4. Craft & embroidery — zardozi, gota, dabka; hours of handwork per piece. 5. Appointments — in-store, video call for NRI families, trunk shows. 6. Real brides — editorial wedding portraits with credit to the look. 7. FAQ — timelines, alterations, delivery, budgets. 8. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Regal top bar: wordmark, Bridal Edit, Trousseau, Craft, Real Brides, "Book Appointment" CTA always visible in gold. Mobile: sticky appointment bar.
- **Key interactions:** Ceremony-based edit filter (tap a ceremony, see the looks); bridal appointment scheduler with date/time slots and video-call option. Trousseau shortlist saves to WhatsApp enquiry.
- **Content blocks:** Ceremony look cards with embroidery hours and fabric; trousseau shortlist; embroidery craft notes (artisan hours per lehenga); appointment types (in-store 60 min, video call 30 min, home trial in select cities); real-bride editorials; timelines (order 8–10 weeks before the wedding).
- **Mobile notes:** Appointment flow is a clean stepper; ceremony filter is a snap-scroll row; shortlist persists; video-call CTA prominent for NRI traffic.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — bride in a red-gold bridal lehenga, palace interior, cinematic light. 2. Product — sangeet lehenga, jewel tones, twirl moment. 3. Product — bridal silk saree drape with zari border. 4. Product — reception gown-lehenga fusion, evening light. 5. Detail — macro of zardozi embroidery / hand-set stones.
- **Differentiation:** Ceremony-first commerce. The appointment is the conversion; every section funnels toward a booked slot. Opulent but organized — gold accents, generous imagery, zero chaos.

- **Conversion goal:** Purchase flagship silk sarees; join the private list for limited loom runs.

The collection — 6–8 signature silks, museum spacing, zari purity stated. 4. Provenance — loom, cluster, Silk Mark number, limited runs. 5. The drape — editorial on wearing silk, styling notes. 6. Private list — first access to limited runs. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Minimal luxury: wordmark, Collection, Provenance, Drape, enquiry icon. Silence is the aesthetic.
- **Key interactions:** Slow cinematic scroll reveals; zari-purity indicator on every product ("4-ply pure zari, tested"); limited-run numbering ("Loom run 12 of 40"). Motion slow and deliberate only.
- **Content blocks:** Manifesto; flagship silk stories with weave data; provenance cards (cluster, loom, Silk Mark); drape and styling editorial; gifting notes; private-list signup.
- **Mobile notes:** Full-bleed dark imagery; single-column museum flow; product data still complete; enquiry one tap away.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — pure silk saree in near-darkness, zari glinting, cinematic. 2. Product — silk saree, studio still life on dark stone. 3. Product — drape detail, border and pallu macro in low light. 4. Product — second colorway, same cinematic treatment. 5. Detail — Silk Mark tag / zari thread macro.
- **Differentiation:** Luxury restraint. Dark cinematic palette, museum spacing, numbered loom runs, zari-purity proof. Sells scarcity and ritual, not variety — the silk answer to the premium coffee maison.

## Cluster C — Contemporary / Edge (attitude-driven, drop + statement)

### design-02-minimal — Modern Minimalist Label
- **Working name (suggested):** Rekha Studio (contemporary pret label)
- **Audience:** 25–40, urban minimalists, workwear and quiet-luxury buyers, design professionals. They buy cut, fabric, and restraint — and hate noise.
- **Conversion goal:** Buy from the core collection (repeat wardrobe staples). Secondary: size/fit confidence via the fit guide.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — pale daylight studio, a single ivory kurta set, enormous whitespace, quiet headline. 2. The philosophy — one paragraph on restraint, fabric honesty. 3. The core — 10–12 timeless pieces, uniform product cards, fabric-first data. 4. Fabric & fit — per-fabric specs (GSM, weave, shrinkage), honest fit guide with model measurements. 5. The palette — seasonal color story, muted. 6. Journal — short notes on cut and cloth. 7. WhatsApp / shop. 8. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Ultra-minimal centered text links with generous letter-spacing; small cart. Calm wins; no sticky CTA shouting.
- **Key interactions:** Colorway switcher with true-fabric swatches (no CGI gloss); fit guide with real model measurements (height, size worn). Slow opacity reveals only.
- **Content blocks:** Philosophy line; core collection with fabric specs on every card; fit guide (model stats, size chart, shrinkage note); palette story; journal notes on construction.
- **Mobile notes:** Single calm column; fit guide one tap from every product; large airy tap areas; no aggressive sticky elements.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — ivory kurta set on a model in pale studio daylight, near-empty frame. 2. Product — charcoal structured kurta, clean product shot. 3. Product — sand-toned co-ord set, editorial daylight. 4. Product — indigo-dyed minimal saree, drape clarity. 5. Detail — seam / stitching macro, fabric texture close-up.
- **Differentiation:** Restraint as luxury. No marquee, no badges, no countdowns. Whitespace is the design; fabric data replaces decoration. The scandi-calm answer in fashion — proof the category holds quiet.

## Image direction (category-wide)
Editorial fashion photography, Indian textiles, real models. Core subjects: draped sarees on real bodies in real light, weavers and looms, natural-dye processes, zari and embroidery macros, atelier hands at work, street-shot editorial, bridal portraiture. Images may show faces — tasteful, editorial, never glamour-stock. No stock-photo gloss: honest skin texture, real fabric behavior, shallow depth of field, natural or motivated light, real locations (looms, streets, palaces, ateliers). The no-visible-face rule applies to VIDEOS only; still images may show faces where editorially appropriate. Each design's shot-list above specifies hero, 2–3 product/look images, and 1 detail image.
