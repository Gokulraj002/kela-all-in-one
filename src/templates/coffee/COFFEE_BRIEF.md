# ATELIER — Coffee & Café Category: Industry Research Brief (Phase 1)

**Purpose:** Ground the 10 coffee designs (design-01 through design-10) in researched industry conventions, then define a UNIQUE direction for each. Research sources include specialty-coffee UX studies, roastery packaging and tasting-card conventions, subscription onboarding patterns, café mobile-intent research, and Scandinavian design dimension analysis.

**Standing truths for the category:**
- Café sites convert visits: hours, address, menu legibility must land within seconds, especially on mobile, where most café discovery happens in the moments before a decision.
- Roastery and premium-brand sites convert trust: origin, altitude, processing method, roast level, and tasting notes are the trust currency. Specific data beats abstract story (e.g. "1,800m, washed, medium-light" beats "carefully sourced").
- Commerce and subscription sites convert through friction removal: choice paralysis is the main enemy. Plan builders, taste quizzes, and transparent pricing/frequency/cancel terms are the proven patterns.
- Never ship a PDF menu, never hide hours, never let decorative motion slow the path to a price or an address.

**Interaction inventory (coffee-specific, keep subtle and premium):** pour/steam animation; bean transitions; roast-level selector; brewing-method picker; origin map; roast timeline; subscription plan builder; horizontal storytelling chapters. Each design below uses at most two, assigned where they serve conversion.

## Cluster A — House / Café (visit-driven, local)

### design-01-artisan — Artisan Coffee House
- **Working name (suggested):** Ember & Oak
- **Audience:** 25–45, design-aware locals, remote workers, weekend explorers. They choose cafés by atmosphere and craft credibility, then check the menu.
- **Conversion goal:** Plan a visit — hours, address, directions. Secondary: browse the menu and confirm it matches their taste.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — sunlit interior with a "today's pour" note (time-of-day aware). 2. Today's board — menu highlights in chalkboard rhythm (Espresso / Filter / Brunch / Bakes tabs). 3. The house — short story of the place, head barista note. 4. Craft — brewing methods and the bar team. 5. Gallery — the room, the light, the regulars. 6. Visit — hours, map, directions, contact. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Minimal top bar: wordmark left, anchor links, "Visit" CTA right. Mobile: sticky bottom bar with two quick actions — "Get directions" and "Menu".
- **Key interactions:** Time-of-day hero note (morning/afternoon/evening copy swaps); menu tab filter; gentle parallax and steam-like soft motion on imagery; never anything that delays the Visit block.
- **Content blocks:** House note from the head barista; menu items with prices and one-line sensory descriptions (descriptive copy lifts perceived value); barista profiles; hours with holiday exceptions; directions and parking note.
- **Mobile notes:** Hours and address visible without scrolling past the hero; menu collapsible by category; tap targets generous; bottom quick-action bar.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — morning sunlight through the café interior, empty table set for the day. 2. Menu — espresso extraction close-up, crema forming. 3. Menu — milk pour forming latte art. 4. Menu — pastry case, croissant detail. 5. Detail — barista hands tamping, shallow depth of field.
- **Differentiation:** Editorial zine rhythm — asymmetric grids, oversized serif headlines, full-bleed daylight photography. The site reads like a printed house journal, not a template.

### design-03-scandi — Scandinavian Café
- **Working name (suggested):** Fika & Ljus
- **Audience:** 25–40, minimalists, café loungers, digital nomads. They come to stay, not to rush.
- **Conversion goal:** Visit and linger — menu and the promise of a calm room. Secondary: catering/private hire inquiry.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — pale daylight interior, enormous whitespace, quiet headline. 2. The fika ritual — short editorial on the daily pause. 3. Menu — grouped by moment (Morning / Fika / Evening), light roasts, bakes. 4. Daylight & seasons — hours framed as light, seasonal menu notes. 5. The room — interior gallery. 6. Visit — address, hours, contact. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Ultra-minimal centered text links with generous letter-spacing; no sticky CTA shouting. Calm wins.
- **Key interactions:** Daylight-aware hero tint (cooler at dawn, warmer at dusk — subtle, CSS only); slow opacity reveals; menu grouped by time-of-day; a "pause" breathing divider animation between sections. Motion must feel like breathing, never theatrical.
- **Content blocks:** Fika explainer; seasonal bakes (cardamom buns, rye); light-roast filter menu; the room's materials (birch, linen, ceramic); slow mornings note for remote workers.
- **Mobile notes:** Single calm column; large airy tap areas; no aggressive sticky elements; hours reachable in one tap from hero.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — pale birch interior in soft daylight, empty and serene. 2. Menu — cardamom bun on ceramic plate. 3. Menu — slow pour-over, glass dripper. 4. Menu — open sandwich on rye. 5. Detail — ceramic cup on linen cloth, window light.
- **Differentiation:** Restraint as luxury. No marquee, no badges, no countdowns. Whitespace is the design; the only animation language is fades and daylight shifts.

### design-04-urban — Urban Coffee
- **Working name (suggested):** Rush Hour Coffee
- **Audience:** 20–35, commuters, city workers, students. They decide in seconds and want speed.
- **Conversion goal:** Order ahead for pickup. Secondary: find the nearest location.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — bold kinetic headline, street energy, order-ahead CTA. 2. Order ahead — pickup flow teaser (menu → time → pay). 3. Locations — list with today's hours per spot. 4. Menu — fast, priced for grab-and-go. 5. Daily rhythm — what's brewing when (morning espresso rush, afternoon cold brew). 6. The city — community, playlists, neighborhood notes. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Bold top bar with sticky "Order ahead" pill CTA that never leaves the viewport.
- **Key interactions:** Marquee ticker of the day's specials; location switcher that updates hours; menu filter (Hot / Cold / Food); high-contrast hover states; quick-add style menu rows. Energy, not elegance.
- **Content blocks:** Pickup order steps; location cards with live-feel hours; grab-and-go menu with prices; loyalty note; city collaborations.
- **Mobile notes:** App-like: bottom tab bar (Menu / Order / Locations); one-tap call; location list first, storytelling last.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — barista at the machine, gritty-chic shop, motion energy. 2. Menu — iced coffee in to-go cup, condensation. 3. Menu — espresso shot pulled fast. 4. Menu — breakfast sandwich, grab-and-go. 5. Detail — shopfront signage / street window at rush hour.
- **Differentiation:** The loud one. Brutalist-leaning type scale, marquee energy, city-first hierarchy. Built for thumbs and train platforms.

### design-05-vintage — Vintage Café
- **Working name (suggested):** The Copper Kettle
- **Audience:** 30–60, nostalgia seekers, heritage lovers, slow travelers. They value continuity and ritual.
- **Conversion goal:** Visit, with table reservation as the premium path. Secondary: browse the unchanged classic menu.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — patina interior, heritage serif, "since 1962" framing. 2. House history — decade timeline (the room, the regulars, the machine). 3. The classic menu — annotated with the year each item joined. 4. The craft — the old lever machine, the unchanged methods. 5. Guestbook — testimonials as handwritten-feel cards. 6. Visit & reserve — reservation form, hours, address. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Classic centered crest wordmark, serif links, letterpress texture feel. Reservation link styled as a printed ticket.
- **Key interactions:** Scrubbable history timeline; menu items with "on the menu since 1974" annotations; sepia/patina photo treatments; reservation modal styled as a table card.
- **Content blocks:** Decade-by-decade story; unchanged classics menu; the machine's story; guestbook; private event note.
- **Mobile notes:** Timeline collapses to vertical; reservation form simplified to date/party/contact; menu readable at small sizes.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — wood-and-brass interior, warm tungsten light. 2. Menu — classic cappuccino in a vintage cup and saucer. 3. Menu — traditional pastry (éclair / sachertorte slice). 4. Menu — brass lever espresso machine detail. 5. Detail — aged menu board / patina close-up.
- **Differentiation:** Heritage as the product. Letterpress typography, sepia grading, timeline-first storytelling. The only design where the past is the hero.

### design-10-experimental — Experimental Café
- **Working name (suggested):** Laboratory No. 9
- **Audience:** 22–40, adventurous tasters, design/art crowd, specialty nerds. They come for the unexpected.
- **Conversion goal:** Book a tasting session. Secondary: explore the current experiment menu.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — immersive sensory image, cryptic headline, "Book a session" CTA. 2. The experiment — current tasting menu framed as a lab protocol. 3. Sensory language — flavor described as color, texture, sound. 4. The lab — methods (siphon, nitro, cascara, fermentation). 5. Sessions — bookable tasting calendar. 6. Manifesto — short, sharp, opinionated. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Minimal avant-garde: hidden/overlay menu, small mono labels, booking CTA always present.
- **Key interactions:** Brewing-method picker with animated transitions between methods; flavor visualizer (abstract, tasteful); session booking flow with date/time slots; unconventional cursor and full-bleed imagery. Daring but never broken.
- **Content blocks:** Rotating experiment menu with batch numbers; method cards; session types (guided flight, deep dive); manifesto; FAQ for first-timers.
- **Mobile notes:** Booking flow collapses to a clean stepper; imagery stays full-bleed; reduce cursor effects, keep motion meaningful.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — dramatic siphon brewer in dark lab light, vapor. 2. Menu — tasting flight of three small glasses. 3. Menu — nitro pour, cascading effect. 4. Menu — abstract latte art, top-down. 5. Detail — macro coffee texture / extraction close-up.
- **Differentiation:** The gallery piece. Dark, sensory, lab-protocol language, booking-first. Polar opposite of the scandi calm — proof the category holds extremes.

## Cluster B — Roastery / Brand (craft credibility, wholesale + retail)

### design-02-roastery — Specialty Roastery
- **Working name (suggested):** Roastworks
- **Audience:** Coffee enthusiasts and home brewers (retail), plus café owners (wholesale). Two audiences, one credibility.
- **Conversion goal:** Buy beans online; wholesale inquiry for B2B.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — roastery drums mid-roast, "roasted this week" freshness promise. 2. Current lineup — product cards with roast level, tasting notes, origin. 3. Roast timeline — the bean-to-bag journey, scroll-animated. 4. Origin map — sourcing regions with farmer cards. 5. Wholesale — tiers and inquiry form. 6. Brew guides — recipes per coffee. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Top bar with shop categories (Single Origins / Blends / Decaf), Wholesale link, cart. Utility-first.
- **Key interactions:** Roast-level selector filtering the lineup; tasting-note badges on every card (origin, process, roast, notes — the tasting-card convention); origin map with clickable pins revealing farmer cards; roast timeline animation; freshness date on every product.
- **Content blocks:** Roast profiles with a roast-scale indicator; per-coffee brew recipes; wholesale tiers (volume, pricing, training); roast-day schedule.
- **Mobile notes:** Product cards stack with badges intact; map degrades to a swipeable region list; wholesale form stays short.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — roastery drum mid-roast, glowing. 2. Product — single-origin bag, flat lay with beans. 3. Product — green-to-roasted bean progression spread. 4. Product — espresso shot with crema, tasting context. 5. Detail — roaster's hands holding fresh-roast beans.
- **Differentiation:** Data-forward craft. Tasting-note badges, roast scales, origin maps, roast-day transparency. The site a coffee nerd bookmarks.

### design-06-premium — Premium Coffee Brand
- **Working name (suggested):** Maison Noir
- **Audience:** 30–55, affluent consumers, gift buyers, connoisseurs. They buy the best and expect the site to feel like it.
- **Conversion goal:** Purchase flagship products; join the private circle (newsletter) for limited releases.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — cinematic dark, single flagship product, one line of copy. 2. The philosophy — craft manifesto, generous whitespace. 3. The collection — 3–4 signature products, museum spacing. 4. Provenance — origin, limited lots, numbered editions. 5. The ritual — brewing it perfectly, editorial. 6. The circle — private list, first access. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Minimal luxury: wordmark, Collection, Provenance, Ritual, cart icon. Silence is the aesthetic.
- **Key interactions:** Slow cinematic scroll reveals; product parallax; limited-edition numbering ("Lot 47 of 200"); gift-wrap option at product level; restrained, slow motion only.
- **Content blocks:** Manifesto; flagship product stories; lot numbering and provenance; ritual guide; gifting; private circle signup.
- **Mobile notes:** Full-bleed imagery; single-column museum flow; simplified but still slow and deliberate.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — flagship bag in dramatic dark light. 2. Product — flagship bag, studio still life. 3. Product — espresso cup, dark moody. 4. Product — bean macro, oil sheen. 5. Detail — embossed seal / gold detail close-up.
- **Differentiation:** Luxury restraint. Dark cinematic palette, museum spacing, numbered lots. Sells scarcity and ritual, not variety.

### design-09-story — Coffee Storytelling
- **Working name (suggested):** From Cherry to Cup
- **Audience:** 25–45, engaged readers, brand-curious, students of craft. They give time, not just clicks.
- **Conversion goal:** Engagement — time on site, chapter completion, newsletter signup at the epilogue.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — chapter title, cinematic farm image, "Begin the journey". 2. Chapter 1: Origin — farmers, altitude, harvest. 3. Chapter 2: Process — washed / natural / honey, interactive diagram. 4. Chapter 3: Roast — the transformation. 5. Chapter 4: Brew — the cup. 6. Chapter 5: People — farmers, roasters, baristas. 7. Epilogue — join the journey (newsletter). 8. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Chapter progress nav — side dots or a top progress bar with chapter names; "next chapter" affordances.
- **Key interactions:** Pinned scroll storytelling with chapter transitions; pull quotes; farmer cards; interactive processing-method diagram (click a method, see the flavor shift); scroll-progress indicator. Horizontal chapter flow on desktop, vertical on mobile.
- **Content blocks:** Long-form chapters with specific data (altitude, varietal, process days); farmer portraits and quotes; process explainer; glossary; newsletter epilogue.
- **Mobile notes:** Chapters stack vertically; diagrams simplify to tap-through cards; progress bar stays visible.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — coffee farm landscape at dawn. 2. Chapter — hands picking red cherries. 3. Chapter — drying beds, beans in sun. 4. Chapter — roast in progress, drum. 5. Detail — cupping table / brewed cup, steam.
- **Differentiation:** Narrative-first. Chapters, progress, pull quotes, farmer protagonists. The only design where reading is the conversion.

## Cluster C — Commerce / Subscription (transaction-driven)

### design-07-subscription — Coffee Subscription
- **Working name (suggested):** Never Empty
- **Audience:** 25–45, home brewers who value freshness and convenience. They fear commitment and choice overload equally.
- **Conversion goal:** Start a subscription — completed plan builder with first delivery date.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — the promise (fresh, on your schedule, pause anytime), plan-builder CTA. 2. How it works — 3 steps. 3. Plan builder — coffee, amount, frequency, grind (live price). 4. Find your match — 2-minute taste quiz teaser. 5. The lineup — what you can subscribe to. 6. Proof — testimonials, ratings. 7. Pricing clarity + FAQ (pause/skip/cancel). 8. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Top bar with persistent "Build your plan" CTA; progress steps inside the builder.
- **Key interactions:** Multi-step plan builder with live price calculation (method → coffee type → amount → grind → frequency); 2-minute taste quiz producing a recommended plan; summary panel with first-delivery date; reassurance microcopy (pause, skip, cancel anytime) at every step.
- **Content blocks:** Plan steps with plain-language options; quiz questions (brew method, taste preference, cups/day); FAQ tackling commitment fears; testimonial strip; gifting option.
- **Mobile notes:** Builder becomes a full-screen stepper; one question per screen; sticky summary bar with price and CTA.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — subscription box arriving / bag on a kitchen counter, morning light. 2. Product — bag lineup, the choice set. 3. Product — beans pouring into a grinder. 4. Product — home brew setup, pour-over. 5. Detail — tasting-notes card included in the box.
- **Differentiation:** Builder-first, not catalog-first. The hero CTA opens the plan wizard; the whole page is a funnel toward one completed configuration. Reassurance copy is a design material.

### design-08-ecommerce — Coffee E-commerce
- **Working name (suggested):** The Whole Shelf
- **Audience:** 25–50, active shoppers comparing options across beans, equipment, and gifts.
- **Conversion goal:** Add to cart and check out. Secondary: wishlist and gift purchase.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — featured collection / seasonal promotion. 2. Shop by category — beans, equipment, subscriptions, gifts. 3. Bestsellers — product grid. 4. Filter toolbar — roast, origin, process, price, sort. 5. Full product grid with taste badges and quick-add. 6. Reviews strip. 7. Brew guides / education. 8. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Category top nav with search and cart drawer; filter toolbar sticky on scroll.
- **Key interactions:** Faceted filters (roast level, origin, process, price) with live counts; sort; quick-add on hover; cart drawer; taste badges on every card; product quick-view modal; wishlist hearts.
- **Content blocks:** Category tiles; product cards (image, name, roast badge, tasting notes, price, quick-add); review highlights; buying guides (grinder picker, brew method matcher); shipping/returns clarity.
- **Mobile notes:** Filters in a slide-in drawer; sticky add-to-cart on quick-view; cart drawer full-screen; checkout-minimal fields.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — styled shelf of products, editorial. 2. Product — bean bag, clean product shot. 3. Product — grinder, equipment shot. 4. Product — gift set, boxed. 5. Detail — packaging close-up, label texture.
- **Differentiation:** Shelf-density commerce. Faceted discovery, quick-add everywhere, cart drawer. The only design optimized for comparison shopping at speed.

## Image direction (category-wide)
Photorealistic, natural light, premium editorial. Core subjects: coffee beans (macro and in-hand), espresso extraction, milk pouring and latte art, barista hands at work, café interiors in real daylight, roastery drums. No stock-photo gloss: shallow depth of field, honest texture, real materials (wood, brass, linen, ceramic). Each design's shot-list above specifies hero, 2–3 menu/product images, and 1 detail image.
