# ATELIER — Creative Agency Category: Industry Research Brief

**Purpose:** Ground 10 agency designs in how real studios win work, then define a UNIQUE
direction for each. Research sources: agency new-business UX studies, portfolio conversion
patterns, RFP/procurement behavior, creative hiring-manager interviews, Awwwards SOTD
case analyses.

**Standing truths for the category:**
- Agency sites convert **inquiries**: the work must prove capability within seconds, and
  the path to "Start a project" must never be more than one scroll away. Vague
  superlatives ("we craft digital experiences") convert nothing — specific outcomes do.
- Hiring decision-makers scan in this order: **work → clients → team → process → contact**.
  Any design that buries the work below three screens of manifesto loses.
- The work image is the product shot. Case imagery must feel like real shipped work —
  art-directed mockups, identity systems in situ, campaign photography — never abstract
  gradient blobs standing in for "creativity."
- Never ship a dead contact form, never hide the studio location, never let a showreel
  autoplay with sound. Motion is the portfolio for motion studios — it must be flawless.

**Interaction inventory (agency-specific):** case-study index with hover/scroll image
reveals; process timeline; showreel player; capability filter; team grid; client marquee;
testimonial slider; project inquiry form (budget selector); journal. Each design uses at
most two, assigned where they serve new business.

## design-01-portfolio — Portfolio-first studio
- **Working name:** Studio Meridian
- **Audience:** CMOs, founders, brand managers comparing studios. They open 12 tabs and
  keep 2.
- **Conversion goal:** Shortlist → inquiry. Secondary: browse 6+ cases.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — full-bleed latest work, one line. 2. Selected work —
  large case grid, filterable by discipline. 3. Capabilities — short list with outcomes.
  4. Clients — logo row / names. 5. Studio — small team note, location. 6. Contact —
  inquiry CTA with budget selector. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Minimal top bar: wordmark, Work, Studio, Contact + "Start a project" CTA.
- **Key interactions:** Case index with scroll-driven image reveal (left list, right image
  crossfades per active row); discipline filter; inquiry form with budget bands.
- **Content blocks:** 6 case studies (client, year, discipline, outcome metric, budget
  band); capability list with one-line proof each; client names; studio facts
  (founded, size, cities); contact with email + form.
- **Mobile notes:** Index becomes stacked cards with images inline; filter becomes
  horizontal chips; inquiry form single column.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — large studio wall with pinned campaign work, daylight.
  2. Case — brand identity mockup stack (stationery, signage). 3. Case — campaign
  photography, street poster in situ. 4. Case — digital product screens, device lineup.
  5. Detail — hands pinning prints during a crit, shallow depth of field.
- **Differentiation:** Work as hero, zero manifesto. The most commercially direct design;
  proof density is the aesthetic.

## design-02-brand — Brand identity agency, process-led
- **Working name:** Mark & Matter
- **Audience:** Founders rebranding, marketing leads. They buy process and rigor.
- **Conversion goal:** Book a discovery call. Secondary: download/see the process.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — identity system flat-lay, "Brands, built to last."
  2. Selected identities — before/after or system spreads. 3. Process — 4 phases with
  deliverables per phase. 4. Capabilities — naming, identity, guidelines, rollout.
  5. Studio — principals, philosophy. 6. Contact — discovery call booking. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Quiet top bar: wordmark, Work, Process, Studio, Contact.
- **Key interactions:** Scrubbed process spine — 4 phases unfold with deliverable
  collages; identity case cards with system spreads.
- **Content blocks:** 5 identity cases (scope, timeline, deliverables); 4-phase process
  with per-phase outputs; naming methodology note; guideline sample spreads; team
  principals; booking form.
- **Mobile notes:** Process becomes vertical stepper; spreads stack.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — identity system flat-lay (cards, stationery, emboss).
  2. Case — logo construction grid, close-up. 3. Case — signage/wayfinding in situ.
  4. Case — packaging system lineup. 5. Detail — hands sketching letterforms, ink.
- **Differentiation:** Process is the product. The most methodical design; every claim
  carries a deliverable.

## design-03-motion — Motion / design film studio, cinematic
- **Working name:** Framehouse
- **Audience:** Creative directors, producers, streaming/brand content leads.
- **Conversion goal:** Watch the reel → request treatment. Secondary: browse films.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — cinematic still, "We make moving images."
  2. Showreel — the film, letterboxed. 3. Selected films — title cards, directors.
  4. Capabilities — title design, brand films, idents, CGI. 5. Studio — directors
  roster, kit. 6. Contact — treatment request. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Minimal dark bar: wordmark, Films, Directors, Contact. Reel CTA.
- **Key interactions:** Pinned showreel section — scroll scrubs through film stills
  with frame counter; letterbox bars widen on scroll.
- **Content blocks:** 5 films (client, director, year, runtime); director roster;
  capability list; studio/kit note; treatment request form.
- **Mobile notes:** Reel section unpins to stacked stills; films list vertical.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — dark studio, projector beam, haze. 2. Film — title
  sequence still, bold type. 3. Film — CGI frame, abstract. 4. Film — behind the
  scenes, camera on set. 5. Detail — editing timeline on a monitor, dark room.
- **Differentiation:** Cinema grammar. Dark, letterboxed, frame-counted. Motion quality
  is judged instantly — every transition must be butter.

## design-04-witty — Copy-driven witty agency
- **Working name:** Frankly®
- **Audience:** Founders and marketers tired of agency-speak. They hire voice.
- **Conversion goal:** "These people get it" → inquiry. Secondary: share a line.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — one killer line, nothing else. 2. The lines — witty
  one-liner cards that toss away on scroll. 3. Work — cases with honest captions.
  4. What we won't do — the anti-list (famous). 5. Studio — small, opinionated bios.
  6. Contact — "Say hello. We reply fast." 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Playful top bar: wordmark, Work, Won't-do, Contact. Copy-first.
- **Key interactions:** Headline toss deck — witty lines stack and fly off on scroll;
  anti-list with strikethrough hover reveals.
- **Content blocks:** 8–10 one-liners; 4 cases with candid captions (what worked, what
  flopped); the won't-do list; team bios with flaws; contact with response-time promise.
- **Mobile notes:** Toss deck becomes swipeable cards; anti-list stacks.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — studio wall of pinned headline cards, playful.
  2. Case — campaign with bold copy, billboard in situ. 3. Case — social campaign
  phone mockups. 4. Case — packaging with funny copy. 5. Detail — marker on paper,
  mid-headline.
- **Differentiation:** Copy is the design. The funniest site in the set; typography
  carries what imagery doesn't.

## design-05-swiss — Minimal Swiss studio, grid discipline
- **Working name:** Norm Studio
- **Audience:** Architects, cultural institutions, editors. They hire restraint.
- **Conversion goal:** View portfolio PDF / inquiry. Secondary: read the journal.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — grid, one line, one image. 2. Index — numbered work
  list, strict rows. 3. Capabilities — two columns, no adjectives. 4. Studio —
  facts, no story. 5. Journal — 3 notes. 6. Contact — address, email, hours.
  7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Hairline top bar: index numbers, tiny caps links. No CTA button —
  email link is the CTA.
- **Key interactions:** Grid-line system — rules draw on scroll, blocks snap to grid;
  numbered index rows with hard rectangular image wipes.
- **Content blocks:** 8 indexed projects (number, client, year, discipline); capability
  list; studio facts (founded, staff, address); 3 journal notes; contact block.
- **Mobile notes:** Grid collapses to single column; index rows remain strict.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — minimal studio interior, white, one poster.
  2. Case — editorial spread, strict grid. 3. Case — poster series on a wall.
  4. Case — exhibition graphics, architectural. 5. Detail — paper stack, crop marks,
  ruler.
- **Differentiation:** Discipline as luxury. The quietest, strictest design; the grid
  is visible and load-bearing.

## design-06-maximal — Bold maximalist collective
- **Working name:** Studio Louder
- **Audience:** Music labels, festivals, streetwear, youth brands. They hire energy.
- **Conversion goal:** "This is the vibe" → DM-style contact. Secondary: follow socials.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — collage explosion, kinetic type. 2. Work — overlapping
  poster avalanche. 3. Crew — collective members, loud portraits. 4. Services —
  shouted list. 5. Manifesto — short, caps. 6. Contact — big email, socials.
  7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Sticker-style bar: rotated badges, loud links. Chaos, aligned.
- **Key interactions:** Collage avalanche — work posters tumble in with rotation on
  scroll; scroll-velocity scatter on the hero collage.
- **Content blocks:** 8 loud cases (posters, covers, merch); crew grid; service
  shouts; manifesto; contact with big type email.
- **Mobile notes:** Avalanche becomes vertical stack with rotation kept small;
  type scales down but stays loud.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — wall of layered posters, wheatpaste texture.
  2. Case — album cover art, bold. 3. Case — festival identity, crowd shot.
  4. Case — merch/streetwear flat-lay. 5. Detail — hands tearing layered prints.
- **Differentiation:** Controlled chaos. The loudest design; more is more, but every
  layer is placed, not random.

## design-07-photo — Photography-led studio
- **Working name:** Halide Studio
- **Audience:** Brands needing campaign photography, editors, art directors.
- **Conversion goal:** View series → book a shoot. Secondary: print sales.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — one perfect frame, full-bleed. 2. Series — photo
  essays with captions. 3. Selected commissions — client work. 4. Process — the
  shoot, film to final. 5. Studio — photographers, darkroom note. 6. Contact —
  booking inquiry. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Quiet dark bar: wordmark, Series, Commissions, Contact.
- **Key interactions:** Darkroom develop — images "develop" from blur/dark into
  clarity on scroll; series presented as filmstrips.
- **Content blocks:** 4 photo series (12+ frames described); 4 commissions;
  process (brief → shoot → edit → deliver); photographer bios; booking form
  (date, usage, budget).
- **Mobile notes:** Filmstrips become swipe carousels; develop effect kept subtle.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — cinematic portrait, dramatic light. 2. Series —
  editorial fashion frame. 3. Series — documentary street frame. 4. Commission —
  campaign still life. 5. Detail — print emerging in developer tray, darkroom red.
- **Differentiation:** Photography is the interface. Darkest image-led design; the
  develop effect makes scrolling feel like the darkroom.

## design-08-digital — Digital product agency
- **Working name:** Interface/Dept
- **Audience:** Product founders, CTOs, VCs. They hire systems, not vibes.
- **Conversion goal:** Book a product audit. Secondary: read a case study.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — product UI montage, metric-led headline.
  2. Outcomes — metric band (conversion lifts, ratings). 3. Case studies —
  systematic rows with metrics. 4. Capabilities — research, design, build, measure.
  5. Process — 4 sprints. 6. Team — leads. 7. Contact — audit booking. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Sharp top bar: wordmark, Work, Process, Contact + "Book an audit" CTA.
- **Key interactions:** Spec-sheet rows — cases expand on scroll with metrics
  counting up; sticky side rail shows active case number.
- **Content blocks:** 5 cases (metrics, timeline, stack); metric band; capability
  grid; sprint process; team leads; audit booking form.
- **Mobile notes:** Rows become cards; metrics stay prominent; rail hidden.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — device lineup with product UI, studio light.
  2. Case — app screens, clean presentation. 3. Case — dashboard UI close-up.
  4. Case — design system components. 5. Detail — stylus wireframing on tablet.
- **Differentiation:** Systems thinking. The most metric-driven design; every case
  leads with a number.

## design-09-indie — Indie freelancer, intimate
- **Working name:** June Park® — independent designer
- **Audience:** Founders who want the person, not the agency. They hire taste + trust.
- **Conversion goal:** Intro call. Secondary: read the notes.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — portrait + "I'm June. I design brands & websites."
  2. Selected work — 6 projects, personal captions. 3. How I work — 1:1 process,
  availability. 4. Notes — short journal entries. 5. About — the person, the desk,
  the dog. 6. Contact — email, calendar link. 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Personal top bar: name, Work, Notes, Contact. Warm.
- **Key interactions:** Journal scroll — margin notes and small photos fade in the
  margins as you read; hand-drawn underline draws under key lines.
- **Content blocks:** 6 projects with personal captions (what I learned); working
  style (calls, async, timelines); availability calendar note; 3 journal notes;
  about with portrait; contact with direct email.
- **Mobile notes:** Margin notes become inline asides; portrait smaller.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — designer portrait, natural light, real desk behind.
  2. Project — brand identity on a laptop, home studio. 3. Project — website on
  screen, cozy setup. 4. Project — printed work held in hands. 5. Detail — desk
  vignette: notebook, coffee, morning light.
- **Differentiation:** Intimacy. First-person voice throughout; the smallest scale
  and the warmest tone in the set.

## design-10-playground — Experimental interactive playground
- **Working name:** PROTO®
- **Audience:** Peers, awards juries, adventurous clients. They come to play.
- **Conversion goal:** "Remember us" → contact. Secondary: play with the type tool.
- **Section order:** 1. Hero — kinetic type field, interactive. 2. Experiments —
  5 interactive sketches. 3. Selected work — the commercial proof. 4. Type tool —
  visitor plays with the variable font. 5. Studio — the humans. 6. Contact —
  "Bring us something weird." 7. Footer.
- **Nav pattern:** Overlay nav: tiny button, full-screen menu. Play first.
- **Key interactions:** Cursor-reactive kinetic field — headline letters scatter and
  reassemble with scroll velocity + pointer; type tool with weight/width sliders.
- **Content blocks:** 5 experiments (WebGL-free: canvas/CSS/GSAP); 4 commercial
  cases; type playground; studio note; contact with playful brief form.
- **Mobile notes:** Kinetic field simplified (fewer letters, no cursor); type tool kept.
- **Image shot-list:** 1. Hero — abstract chromatic light, experimental.
  2. Experiment — kinetic type poster. 3. Experiment — interactive installation.
  4. Case — bold campaign, weird. 5. Detail — hands on a keyboard, screen glow,
  code-ish.
- **Differentiation:** The toy. The only design where interaction IS the content;
  commercial work is the footnote, not the headline.

## Image direction (category-wide)
Photorealistic, art-directed, real studio life — never corporate stock. Core subjects:
studio interiors in real daylight, pin-up/crit walls, hands at work (sketching, pinning,
cutting, shooting), printed work in situ (posters, stationery, signage), screens showing
real-looking UI, darkroom and film craft. Shallow depth of field, honest texture, real
materials (paper, ink, wheatpaste, film, screens). No people with visible faces except
09's portrait and 06's crew (backs/side profiles preferred elsewhere). No text in
generated images that must stay legible — abstract or blurred type only.
